## E. CHAMPION

- **Theme:** The AI Layoff Story Changed — Your Risk Didn't
- **Subtitle:** In 2026, AI went from the top reason companies gave for layoffs to almost none in
  six months. What that swing reveals about corporate messaging, and how to measure your real
  exposure instead.
- **Thesis:** Layoff reasons are what companies say, not a measurement of what AI does. The viewer's
  risk is set by their role's exposure and the options they have built, not by which story dominates
  the headlines this month.
- **Belief vs. truth:** Belief — "AI is wiping out jobs" (or, now, "the AI scare is over"). Truth —
  AI was the stated reason for ~20% of announced cuts this year, its share swung from 7% to 40% to
  ~3%, and the tracker itself says citing AI can be aimed at investors.
- **Contradiction:** the year AI-cited cuts more than doubled 2025's total is also the year total
  announced cuts fell 44%.
- **Mechanism:** a layoff announcement is a corporate communication with several audiences
  (investors, employees, regulators); the stated reason follows what each audience rewards.
  [ProfitMoments Interpretation, supported by F08.]
- **Human consequence:** reacting to the headline — panic or relief — instead of auditing one's own
  position; years on autopilot until the decision is made by someone else.
- **Rationale — why now, why it wins:** the October data (2 weeks old) completes the arc; demand for
  "ai layoffs" is growing (+60.7%) and long-form outliers show the topic converts attention (27x,
  2.2x on 283K views); most competing videos sell the apocalypse, leaving the calm, data-led
  reinterpretation open; the AI & Power Shift line is unused. D2 has solid evidence but declining
  demand and a crowded alarm category; D3 repeats existing work.
- **Estimated runtime:** 9–11 minutes — one arc, 5 spoken facts, one mechanism, one practical
  implication. M22 confirms against retention and production cost.
- **Uncertainty:** whether the swing reflects messaging, real adoption timing, regulation, or a mix
  is not measured by any source here.
- **Limits:** announced cuts ≠ actual layoffs; stated reasons ≠ causes; U.S.-based employers only.
- **Prohibited claims:** "AI layoffs were fake / an excuse / a lie"; "AI isn't taking jobs"; "your job
  is safe"; naming any specific company as using AI as a pretext; any prediction of future layoffs;
  presenting vidIQ figures as audience facts.

**Sequence:** belief → fact → mechanism → consequence → reinterpretation → implication (see D1).
