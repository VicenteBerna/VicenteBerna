## Theme

**The AI Layoff Story Changed — Your Risk Didn't**

*Subtitle:* In 2026, AI went from the top reason companies gave for layoffs to almost none in six
months. What that swing reveals about corporate messaging, and how to measure your real exposure
instead.

**Thesis:** Layoff reasons are what companies say, not a measurement of what AI does. The viewer's
risk is set by their role's exposure and the options they have built, not by which story dominates
the headlines this month.

**Editorial line:** AI & Power Shift · **Journey:** Employability · **Type:** HYBRID (NOW + EVERGREEN)

## Retention sequence

1. **Belief:** "AI is wiping out jobs" — or, now, "the AI scare is over."
2. **Fact:** AI was cited for 40% of announced cuts in May (record), ~3% in October.
3. **Mechanism:** a layoff announcement is a corporate message with several audiences; the stated
   reason follows what those audiences reward. *(ProfitMoments interpretation, supported by the
   tracker's own analysis — label it as interpretation.)*
4. **Consequence:** the headline tells you the message, not your risk.
5. **Reinterpretation:** AI won't replace everyone — but it exposes who has options.
6. **Implication:** measure your own exposure and build options now, while employed.

**Contradiction to use:** the year AI-cited cuts more than doubled 2025's total is also the year total
announced cuts fell 44%.

**Human consequence (the ProfitMoments lens):** reacting to headlines — panic or relief — instead of
auditing one's own position; years on autopilot until someone else makes the decision.
