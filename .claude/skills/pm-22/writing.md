# Motor 22 — Writing rules: hook, retention, ElevenLabs

These rules come from the first ProfitMoments script review: its weak hook, its early caveats and its
six consecutive paragraphs of statistics were the reasons it scored 6.5 instead of 8.5.

## The hook — the first 15 seconds

- About **35 to 40 words** (15 seconds at ~150 wpm).
- It does three jobs: **confirms the click** the title promised, **opens a question** the viewer
  cannot close yet, and **shows what is at stake for the viewer** ("you" early).
- Write **3 to 5 hook options**, assess each qualitatively (clarity, stake, curiosity, title fit,
  truth), keep the best one, and show the runner-up in the report.
- No channel intro, no "in this video", no subscribe pitch, no caveat in the hook.

## Limits and caveats — where they go

- A limit that **changes the meaning** of a claim lives inside the claim's own sentence (the
  handoff's SPOKEN CLAIMS already carry it).
- The material limits of the whole video are stated **once, briefly, right after the Viewer
  Contract** — around the first minute. One or two sentences, confident, not apologetic.
- Everything else goes to the description (SOURCE NOTES).
- Never repeat a caveat already made. Brutal honesty means saying the truth firmly, not hedging
  every sentence.

## Retention structure

- **At most 2 consecutive data-heavy paragraphs.** After two, turn: a question, a contrast, a human
  mirror, or the mechanism.
- A **reset every 60 to 90 seconds** (~150–225 words): a new question, a turn in the argument, or a
  payoff of an earlier loop.
- A **mid-video re-hook at 35–40% of the runtime**: restate what is still unanswered and why it
  matters now.
- Open loops must close. Map each loop to the paragraph that pays it off.
- The promise-fulfillment point is named in the retention plan and happens before the closing.

## ElevenLabs-ready narration

Write for a synthetic voice reading it right the first time — every regeneration costs credits.

- **Percentages and decimals in words:** "forty percent", "four point one percent".
- **No symbols:** write "dollars", "percent", "and" — never $, %, &.
- **Large numbers:** digits with commas are fine ("38,579"); round only when the rounded form is
  still honest and the handoff does not require the exact figure ("nearly thirty-nine thousand").
- **No ambiguous abbreviations:** "the third quarter" not "Q3"; spell out agency names on first use
  if the voice may stumble ("the Bureau of Labor Statistics"). "AI" is fine.
- **Dates spoken naturally:** "in May", "this October", "last year".
- **Pauses through punctuation:** a period for a full stop, an em dash for a beat, a new paragraph
  for a breath. No ellipses chains, no parentheses, no brackets.
- **Sentence length:** mostly 8–20 words; vary rhythm; avoid tongue-twisters and stacked
  prepositional phrases.
- **Pronunciation notes** for names or terms the voice may misread go in the report (and later in
  the M33 handoff), never inside the narration.

## Voice and tone

Calm, grave, documentary. Intelligent, confident, restrained — premium editorial. The ProfitMoments
lens: translate the cold number into time, family, identity and freedom, without inventing people or
anecdotes.
